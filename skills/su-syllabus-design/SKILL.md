# Syracuse University Syllabus Design Skill

## Purpose

Provide evidence-based guidance for designing or improving undergraduate syllabi at Syracuse University. This skill integrates three complementary frameworks: backward design methodology, learning science research, and inclusive teaching practices.

## Core Design Frameworks

### Backward Design (Wiggins & McTighe)

**Three Stages:**
1. **Identify desired results** — Define clear, measurable learning outcomes
2. **Determine acceptable evidence** — Design assessments that demonstrate outcome achievement
3. **Plan learning experiences** — Create activities that prepare students for assessments

### Learning Outcome Taxonomies

**Bloom's Taxonomy** — Use action verbs: Remember/Understand (define, explain, summarize); Apply/Analyze (demonstrate, compare, differentiate); Evaluate/Create (critique, justify, design, construct).

**Fink's Taxonomy** — Foundational Knowledge, Application, Integration, Human Dimension, Caring, Learning How to Learn.

### Universal Design for Learning (UDL)

| UDL Principle | Syllabus Application |
|---------------|---------------------|
| Multiple Means of Engagement | Connect to real problems; offer topic/format choices |
| Multiple Means of Representation | Varied formats; accessible materials |
| Multiple Means of Action/Expression | Allow demonstration options |

### Learning Science Principles

- Design sessions in 10-15 minute segments with processing breaks
- Build in frequent low-stakes retrieval practice
- Use spaced practice—revisit concepts across the semester
- Answer "Why should I care?" explicitly for content and assignments
- Emphasize growth mindset

## Syllabus Review Checklist

### Design & Alignment
- [ ] Learning outcomes clearly stated with action verbs
- [ ] Each assessment connects to specific outcomes
- [ ] Activities prepare students for assessments

### Learning Science
- [ ] Course structure accounts for attention (chunked content)
- [ ] Low-stakes retrieval practice incorporated
- [ ] Relevance and purpose communicated

### Universal Design
- [ ] Multiple engagement strategies
- [ ] Multiple representation formats
- [ ] Multiple expression options

### Inclusive Excellence
- [ ] Tone is warm, welcoming, and encouraging
- [ ] Diversity/inclusion statement present
- [ ] Policies include structured flexibility
- [ ] Campus resources listed with warm language

### Student-Centeredness
- [ ] Written in second person or first-person plural
- [ ] Explains "why" for content and policies
- [ ] Communicates instructor commitment to success

## Application Workflows

**Creating a New Syllabus:** Define outcomes → design assessments → plan activities → draft components → apply inclusive practices → review against checklist.

**Reviewing an Existing Syllabus:** Audit with checklist → prioritize improvements → revise systematically → student perspective test.

## Tone Transformation Examples

| Cold/Punitive | Warm/Supportive |
|---------------|-----------------|
| "Students must contact me via email only." | "I welcome you to reach out via email or during office hours." |
| "Late work will not be accepted." | "Please communicate with me if you need an extension." |
| "Attendance is mandatory." | "Your participation enriches our discussions." |
